# Clinic Interview Q&A (Q1–Q32)

Source: interview with Dr. Dana Roxas, clinic owner. Answers are recorded as given; incomplete or unclear answers are marked rather than filled in.

The historical 31-module planning list from the original planning notes is intentionally excluded; the canonical modules are M1–M25 (see CLAUDE_OPERATING_MODE.md).

## Q1. How long has the clinic been operating, and what dental services do you currently offer (e.g., general dentistry, orthodontics, oral surgery, cosmetic dentistry)?

**Answer:** Since 1981. Services include general dentistry, orthodontics, oral surgery, and radiographic imaging.

## Q2. How many branches or locations does the clinic have, and where are they located?

**Answer:** 3 branches: 2 in Bocaue and 1 in Guiguinto.

## Q3. What is your role at the clinic, and how long have you been practicing here?

**Answer:** Owner, since 1981. I do the management and decision-making in the practice, aside from treating patients.

## Q4. How many staff does the clinic have in total (dentists, dental assistants, front desk/receptionist, etc.)?

**Answer:** 12 dentists and 7 staff, consisting of a mix of assistants and receptionists.

## Q5. How many dentists or hygienists are actively seeing patients on a given day?

**Answer:** We maintain at least 2 dentists per day per clinic.

## Q6. Is your current staffing generally sufficient for patient volume, or are there times you feel short-staffed?

**Answer:** There are days when, because of patient volume, we feel short-staffed because we want to accommodate as many patients as we can.

## Q7. Roughly how many patients do you see per day?

**Answer:** Estimated at least 10 to 20 patients per day.

## Q8. Roughly how many patients do you see per week, and per month?

**Answer:** Estimated at around 30 to 50 patients per week. A monthly figure was not provided.

## Q9. Has patient volume grown, stayed the same, or dropped over the past year? By roughly how much?

**Answer:** The patient/client base has expanded throughout the years. No specific percentage was provided.

## Q10. Are there peak days or times (e.g., weekends, after office hours) or seasonal spikes where patient volume increases significantly?

**Answer:** Weekends usually have more patients, especially from October to February.

## Q11. Roughly how much is the clinic's gross revenue per day, per week, or per month?

**Answer:** Approximately ₱15,000 daily.

## Q12. What is the typical price range for your most common procedures?

**Answer:** Cleaning: ₱1,000–₱1,500; Extraction: ₱10,000–₱15,000; Filling: ₱800–₱2,000.

## Q13. What are your biggest recurring operating costs?

**Answer:** Salaries and dental supplies take up a large part of expenses, as well as maintenance.

## Q14. Do you currently pay for any dental practice management software, booking tools, or subscriptions? Roughly how much per month?

**Answer:** Yes. The clinic pays around ₱1,400 per month for software.

## Q15. Can you walk us through what happens from the moment a patient books an appointment to when they leave after treatment?

**Answer:** Once a patient enters, they are assigned to a dentist at the patient lounge. Staff prepare the necessary equipment, treatment is performed, and during payment the patient's next appointment is arranged. The final phrase in the original response was incomplete.

## Q16. Which steps are currently manual or paper-based?

**Answer:** All forms from booking to payment are digitized. The only identified exception is the prescription.

## Q17. How do you currently manage patient records and treatment history?

**Answer:** Through digital software.

## Q18. Do you use any existing software or application for scheduling, billing, or patient records today? If so, what do you like and dislike about it?

**Answer:** Yes. The clinic likes the current software because it is centralized, which is useful because the practice has multiple branches.

## Q19. What part of your process causes the most delays?

**Answer:** Long patient queues.

## Q20. What mistakes or errors happen most often?

**Answer:** Scheduling conflicts.

## Q21. What complaints do you receive most often from patients?

**Answer:** Long waiting time.

## Q22. How do you currently handle HMO or insurance claims processing, and is this a difficult part of your workflow?

**Answer:** Yes. It adds another step to the procedure, and it can take a long time for HMO companies to respond.

## Q23. If you could automate or fix just one part of your current workflow, what would it be and why?

**Answer:** HMO contacting and online messaging on the clinic's social media.

## Q24. Would a digital appointment booking, scheduling, and patient records system be something the clinic would realistically consider adopting?

**Answer:** Yes.

## Q25. Is there anyone else on your team we should also speak with for a more complete picture?

**Answer:** Yes.

## Q26. Would you be open to a short follow-up conversation once we have an initial design or prototype?

**Answer:** Yes.

## Q27. For each major step (booking, check-in, treatment, billing, follow-up), who specifically is responsible?

**Answer:** Booking, check-in, billing, and follow-up are handled by the receptionist. Treatment is handled by the dentist with support from the assistant.

## Q28. What information is needed to start a booking, and what is the end result once a patient leaves?

**Answer:** For booking: patient information, chief complaint, available time, and dentist schedule. The response also identified the resulting appointment schedule and dentist availability. The original answer was brief and partially fragmented.

## Q29. Which of your daily steps follow a fixed, predictable rule versus steps that require staff judgment or case-by-case decisions?

**Answer:** Appointments are fixed. Treatment duration and treatment decisions are handled case by case.

## Q30. Has the clinic tried new tools or systems before? How did staff react?

**Answer:** For the digital system, there was a learning curve for everyone, including adapting to the integration of computers into the practice.

## Q31. Who would be responsible for maintaining or troubleshooting a new system day-to-day, and how reliable is your internet/tech setup?

**Answer:** The clinic's internet is reliable. For the current software, maintenance/training involved skill-based training. The exact day-to-day person responsible for troubleshooting was not specified.

## Q32. What would 'better' look like to you in measurable terms?

**Answer:** Shorter wait times and more real-time updates in booking and to patients prior to treatments.
